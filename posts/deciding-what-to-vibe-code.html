What should your firm actually build? Beyond tokenmaxxing

Nov 2, 2026

So you rolled out Claude or ChatGPT; what’s next? Using PE examples given my background.

In my experience, firms both underestimate and overestimate what can — and should — be built internally.

They underestimate what employees could build if they had the right tools, data access, permissions, infrastructure and support (beyond Claude Code tokens) to turn an AI-generated prototype into production-ready software.

An associate builds a sourcing tool over a weekend. It works, but it needs a Salesforce API key compliance won't issue to a script nobody has reviewed, and a server to run on instead of their laptop. So every Monday they run it on their laptop, then have Claude log into Salesforce with their own credentials and upload the results. Until they get staffed on a deal, and the Mondays stop.

They also overestimate what is worth building. Employees who successfully build something useful can end up spending their time maintaining and debugging bespoke, vibe-coded tools rather than focusing on their core responsibilities.

Another associate builds a dashboard for tracking deal flow. It’s good, so the team asks for a pipeline view, then a filter by sector, then an export for the Monday meeting. Six months in, the associate is maintaining a homegrown BI tool with a dozen users and a backlog, and spending Fridays on feature requests instead of deals.

Blanket access to Claude Code was the right call for the exploration phase (the tokenmaxxing era). But making it useful requires firms to answer a question they have mostly skipped: what do we actually want to own?

My take is to buy the plumbing and let employees build the alpha. In other words, buy standardized capabilities that are better (and/or more cheaply) provided by specialists, and build the differentiated applications where the firm's proprietary knowledge, data and judgment create an advantage.

In sourcing, for example, buy the software that finds companies, identifies contacts, adds them to the CRM and runs standard outreach sequences. Let employees build bespoke agents that identify companies matching specific investment theses, e.g. monitoring job postings in a particular geography for companies hiring their first enterprise sales representative.

In other cases, the plumbing might simply be a general-purpose AI model. Use Claude or ChatGPT to read a data room, then let employees build the visualizations, financial models and analytical tools that reflect how the firm evaluates an investment.

The lesson from the exploration phase isn't to pull back on building. It's to be deliberate about where it happens. Think less Silicon Valley, more Beijing: identify the areas that matter, concentrate infrastructure, access and support there, and let people build around those priorities while importing the rest. The firms that get the most out of AI won't be the ones that build the most. They'll be the ones that know where to point their builders.
